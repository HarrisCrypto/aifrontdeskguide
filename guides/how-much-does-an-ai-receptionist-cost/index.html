How Much Does an AI Receptionist Cost in 2026? Real Pricing Compared

AI receptionist pricing in 2026, verified October 2026: Goodcall from $79/mo with unlimited minutes, Vapi from $0.05/min plus provider costs. Compare billing models and learn what actually drives your bill.

By Jason O’Keefe·October 12, 2026·Updated October 17, 2026

How we price-checked this guide: every plan, price, and overage rate below was read from the vendors’ own pricing pages or current third-party pricing breakdowns on October 16, 2026, and the Goodcall plans re-verified on October 18, 2026. Prices change — confirm on the vendor’s site before you buy. This page contains affiliate links; see our affiliate disclosure.

The short answer

An AI receptionist — software that answers your business phone line, talks to callers, books appointments, and takes messages around the clock — costs roughly $79 to $300 per month for most small businesses in 2026, depending on which billing model you choose. But the sticker price is the least informative number on the page. What you actually pay depends on the billing model: per-unique-caller plans and per-minute platforms behave very differently at the same call volume. We’ll prove it with math below.

The billing models, explained

A “unique customer” is a distinct phone number that interacts with the agent in a given month — if the same customer calls ten times, they count once. Robocalls and hang-ups that never engage the agent don’t count.

The key property: minutes are unmetered. A 20-minute emergency intake and a 30-second wrong number cost exactly the same. If your average call runs long — trades, home services, anything with real conversations — this is the plan shape to compare everything else against.

What $79 actually buys: 100 unique callers, unlimited talk time, 24/7 answering, one scripted call-handling flow, SMS follow-up links. No setup fee on published plans.

Real 2026 pricing: Vapi Try Vapi

Vapi is a developer platform, so its pricing reads like infrastructure — because it is.

  • Orchestration: $0.05 per minute. This is Vapi’s fee for running the call.
  • Everything else, passed through at cost: speech-to-text, the language model, text-to-speech voice, and telephony are each billed at the provider’s listed price, with no markup from Vapi.
  • Realistic all-in: Vapi’s own pricing calculator puts a typical stack (Deepgram + OpenAI + ElevenLabs) at $0.08–$0.13 per minute. Independent breakdowns estimate $0.07–$0.25 per minute depending on model and voice choices.
  • Extras: extra concurrent lines around $10/month each and ~$2 per phone number have been reported; a HIPAA compliance add-on runs $2,000/month. Plan names and packaging have shifted during 2026 — verify the current structure on their pricing page.

There is no “plan” in the traditional sense on the usage-based tier: you pay for what you use, so there’s no overage cliff — but also no ceiling. A quiet month costs almost nothing; a spike month just bills more minutes.

The math: a 100-call month on each

Assume a home-services shop: 100 calls in a month, 6 minutes average (longer than average — that’s the point of the example), 70 unique callers.

Goodcall (Starter, $79): 70 unique callers is under the 100 cap. Minutes don’t matter. Bill: $79. If a freeze week brings 130 unique callers, it’s $79 + (30 × $0.79) = $102.70.

Vapi: 100 calls × 6 minutes = 600 minutes. Orchestration: 600 × $0.05 = $30. All-in at $0.08–$0.13/min: $48–$78. At the higher third-party estimate ($0.25/min): $150. Plus any line/number fees, plus whoever built and maintains the agent.

Notice what happened: at long call lengths, the per-caller plan wins on predictability, and the per-minute platform’s real cost depends heavily on which models you picked. At short call lengths (2-minute average = 200 minutes), Vapi lands around $16–$50 in platform cost — cheaper than Goodcall’s $79 floor, if the build labor is already covered.

The rule: long calls and repeat callers → per-caller billing. Short calls and in-house technical capacity → per-minute platforms. Model your real call lengths, not the vendor’s example.

What actually drives the bill

  1. Average call length. The single biggest variable. Time three real calls before you buy anything.
  2. Billing model mismatch. Per-minute billing on a long-call business (or per-caller billing on a high-new-caller business) is how $79 plans become $300 bills.
  3. Volume spikes. Storm season, freeze season, promo weeks. Price your peak month.
  4. Model and voice choices (Vapi). A premium voice and a frontier model can triple the per-minute provider cost versus budget defaults.
  5. Concurrency and numbers (Vapi). Extra simultaneous lines and phone numbers add fixed monthly fees on top of usage.
  6. Add-ons. HIPAA compliance, zero-retention, enterprise SLAs — five-figure annual territory on developer platforms. Irrelevant for most local businesses; critical for regulated ones.
  7. The build (Vapi). The invoice nobody puts on the pricing page: engineering time to build, test, and maintain the agent.

The bottom line

If you want a finished product with a bill you can predict: Goodcall, from $79/month, unlimited minutes Try Goodcall. If you have technical capacity and want per-minute economics: Vapi, from $0.05/min plus provider costs — realistically $0.08–$0.25/min all-in Try Vapi. Either way, time your real calls, model your peak month, and confirm the current price on the vendor’s page before you sign. The cheapest plan is the one whose billing model matches your phone.

Frequently asked questions

What is the typical monthly cost of an AI receptionist in 2026?

For the services we track, entry pricing runs $79/month (Goodcall, 100 unique callers, unlimited minutes) while developer platforms like Vapi start at $0.05/minute for orchestration plus provider costs passed through — realistically $0.08–$0.25/minute all-in. Your actual bill depends more on the billing model than the sticker price.

Do AI receptionists charge per minute or per call?

Both models exist, plus a third: per unique caller. Goodcall bills per unique customer per month with unlimited minutes. Vapi bills per minute ($0.05 orchestration plus transcription, model, voice, and telephony at cost). The right model depends on your call volume and average call length — long calls favor per-caller billing.

What is the real all-in cost of Vapi per minute?

Vapi advertises $0.05/minute, but that is only the orchestration fee. Speech-to-text, the language model, text-to-speech, and telephony are billed on top at provider cost. Vapi's own calculator puts a typical stack at $0.08–$0.13/minute; independent estimates run $0.07–$0.25/minute depending on model and voice choices.

Are there setup fees for AI receptionists?

Goodcall lists no setup fee on its published plans — it is self-serve software. Vapi has no setup fee either, but the real "setup cost" is whoever builds the agent: your time or an agency's invoice. Done-for-you services in this category commonly charge four-figure setup fees; always confirm on the pricing page before you buy.

What happens when I go over my plan limits?

On Goodcall, each unique customer past your cap costs 79¢ on Starter, 26¢ on Growth, and 15¢ on Scale. On Vapi's usage-based billing there is no cap to exceed — you simply pay for every minute, so a spike month just produces a bigger invoice. Model your peak month, not your average one.

How often do AI receptionist prices change?

Often enough that you should verify on the vendor's pricing page before signing — Vapi's plan packaging has shifted during 2026.
