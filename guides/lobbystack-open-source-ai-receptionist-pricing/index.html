LobbyStack: The Open-Source AI Receptionist at $30 a Month — 150 Minutes, Then 20¢ a Minute

LobbyStack is the only open-source AI receptionist we have verified: $30 a month for 150 minutes on Starter, $100 for 500 on Pro, with a 14-day free trial. At about 100 three-minute calls it costs $60 a month — the cheapest verified entry in the queue — but call summaries live in the dashboard, not on your phone.

By Jason O’Keefe·November 22, 2026

How we checked this guide: every LobbyStack price, minute allowance, and feature below was read from LobbyStack’s own site (lobbystack.com) on November 22, 2026. The Goodcall, Rosie, and Dialzara figures are the vendor-verified prices already published in our cost guide. Disclosure: this site is run by the founder of OplyAI, an AI front-desk company. This page contains affiliate links; see our affiliate disclosure.

LobbyStack is the cheapest verified entry in AI answering at about 100 calls a month — $60 on Starter — and it is the only open-source receptionist we have verified: buyers can use the hosted cloud or self-host the stack. The trade-off is a real one: call summaries live in the dashboard, not on your phone.

The pricing: free trial, two paid tiers, one custom tier

LobbyStack’s own pricing page lists four tiers, all with unlimited agents and unlimited concurrent calls:

The free plan is a 14-day trial with 30 minutes, one phone number, and one agent — not a permanent free tier. No credit card is required to start it. Enterprise adds multiple dedicated numbers and dedicated implementation support.

Every paid plan includes a dedicated US or Canadian business number, Zapier, webhook, and MCP integrations, and email support (priority email on Pro). LobbyStack answers in 70+ languages, books appointments into Google Calendar, Cal.com, or Calendly and texts the caller a confirmation, transfers urgent calls to a person, and collects caller details for follow-up. It can answer every call or only the ones the team misses.

The math that decides the plan

LobbyStack bills by the minute, so run your actual talk time — not your call count:

The crossover sits just under 200 calls: below it, Starter wins; above it, Pro wins. An independent pricing index computed the same $60 and $172 figures from the vendor’s rates — a useful second check on the arithmetic.

The open-source angle is the story, not just the price

LobbyStack’s own FAQ states it plainly: it is an open-source AI receptionist, and buyers can use LobbyStack Cloud or self-host the stack “when you want more control over infrastructure and customer data.” No other verified vendor in our queue offers a self-host path. That matters for two buyers in particular: a shop that wants to own its call-handling stack rather than rent it, and an agency that wants to host receptionists for clients.

The honest counterweight: LobbyStack is a younger, smaller vendor. Its page publishes no customer count, no review count, and no uptime figure — do not budget on a track record the vendor has not stated. And it is a generalist small-business receptionist, not a trades-specific tool: it books into calendars and transfers to people, but it does not write into Jobber, ServiceTitan, or Housecall Pro directly — only through Zapier, webhooks, or MCP.

The honest limit: summaries stay in the dashboard

LobbyStack’s own FAQ answers the question directly: “Not after every call. LobbyStack saves a summary of each call in your dashboard.” When it takes a message, it sends the team an email alert, and on paid plans you can also turn on SMS alerts. If your workflow is “the owner reads a text after every call,” LobbyStack does not do that — the summary is one dashboard visit away. That is a workflow difference, not a defect, but it decides the purchase for owners who live on their phones.

Two more limits from the vendor’s own page: no rollover is stated for unused minutes, and there is no per-call fee beyond the per-minute rate — do not quote a per-call price, because the vendor publishes none.

The bottom line

LobbyStack is the cheapest verified way into AI answering at low volume — $30 a month for 150 minutes, $60 at about 100 three-minute calls — with the only self-host option in the queue. But it is a generalist, minute-billed product whose call summaries live in a dashboard. If your calls run long, Goodcall Starter at $79 for 100 callers with unlimited minutes Try Goodcall is still the plan to beat; if you want the cheapest metered ladder with warm transfer, Dialzara Pro at $99 for 220 minutes is the comparison. Model your peak month in minutes, not calls, and decide whether the dashboard — not a text — is where you want your summaries.

Frequently asked questions

How much does LobbyStack cost?

LobbyStack's own pricing page, verified November 22, 2026, lists a free 14-day trial (30 voice minutes), Starter at $30 a month ($24 billed annually) for 150 voice minutes, then $0.20 a minute, and Pro at $100 a month ($80 billed annually) for 500 voice minutes, then $0.18 a minute. Enterprise is custom pricing for higher volume.

What does LobbyStack cost at 100 calls a month?

About $60 a month on Starter, at three minutes a call: $30 for the first 150 minutes plus 150 overage minutes at $0.20. At about 300 calls (900 minutes) the Pro plan costs $172 a month: $100 for 500 minutes plus 400 overage minutes at $0.18. Both worked costs match an independent pricing index's own computation.

Is LobbyStack really open source?

Yes. LobbyStack's own FAQ says it is an open-source AI receptionist: buyers can use LobbyStack Cloud (hosted) or self-host the stack for control over infrastructure and customer data. No other verified vendor in our queue offers a self-host path.

Does LobbyStack text you a summary after every call?

No. LobbyStack saves a summary of each call in its dashboard; it does not send a summary to the owner's phone after every call. When it takes a message it sends an email alert, and on paid plans you can also turn on SMS alerts.

How does LobbyStack compare with Goodcall?

Different billing units. Goodcall charges per unique caller — $79 a month for 100 callers, minutes unlimited — while LobbyStack charges per minute of talk time. A shop with short calls is usually cheaper on LobbyStack; a shop with long calls is usually cheaper on Goodcall, because its minutes do not matter.
