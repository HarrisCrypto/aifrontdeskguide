Front Office: the Contractor-Only AI Receptionist at $299–$449 Flat — Email Summaries, Not Calendar Booking

Front Office is a trade-specific AI receptionist built inside a working home-service contractor: $299/month for up to 60 calls, $449 for 61–200, flat rate, live in two business days. The honest gap: it emails call details but does not book into a calendar.

By Jason O’Keefe·October 31, 2026

How we checked this guide: every Front Office price, plan limit, and feature below was read from Front Office’s own homepage (contractorfrontoffice.com) on November 1, 2026. The Goodcall and Frontdesk figures are the vendor-verified prices already published in our cost guide. Disclosure: this site is run by the founder of OplyAI, an AI front-desk company. This page contains affiliate links; see our affiliate disclosure.

Most AI receptionists are built for every business at once. Front Office is built for exactly one kind: the home-service contractor losing after-hours calls.

Front Office (contractorfrontoffice.com) was started inside a working home-service contractor, per its own homepage, and it shows in the design. It answers 24/7 in your company’s name, captures the caller’s name, number, address, and problem, quotes only the service-call fee and hours you set, and emails you the details after every call. It is deliberately narrow — and the narrowness is both the selling point and the catch.

The pricing: flat by call volume, not by minute

Front Office charges a flat monthly rate by how many calls you take, not by how long they last:

PlanCalls includedPrice
StarterUp to 60 calls/month$299/month ($9.80/day framing)
Full Front Office61–200 calls/month$449/month ($14.90/day framing)

No per-minute or per-call charges, no setup fee for founding clients, and founding clients lock their rate for 24 months. A 7-day trial runs free on your own line — nothing is billed until day 7, and only if you keep it.

That flat rate is the honest comparison point against the metered plans in our cost guide. Goodcall Starter is $79/month for 100 unique callers with unlimited minutes Try Goodcall; Frontdesk (My AI Front Desk) is $99/month for 200 voice minutes plus chat and SMS. Front Office’s entry tier costs roughly three to four times either — so the question is never “is it cheap?” but “does the trade-specific setup earn the premium?”

What it actually does on a call

Per the vendor’s own homepage, every call follows the same job:

  • Answers 24/7 in your company’s name, and tells callers it is an AI — no pretending to be a person
  • Captures name, callback number, service address, and what’s wrong, plus urgency
  • Quotes only the service-call fee and hours you set — it never invents a price or promises an arrival time
  • On danger calls (gas smell, smoke, fire, a CO alarm, anyone in danger), tells the caller to get out and call 911
  • Emails the details to the owner after every call

Setup runs from eight answers — hours, services, fee, service area, what counts as an emergency, the greeting, which line to forward, and where to email each call — and the shop goes live within two business days. Before go-live, you hear a recorded test call and approve it. You keep your number via call forwarding: every call, only the calls you miss, or only after-hours, toggled with a dial code. Trades covered on the page: HVAC, plumbing, electrical, roofing, garage doors, restoration, pest control, septic and drain, appliance repair, and generators. You can hear it live on the demo line published on the vendor’s page: (609) 536-1044.

The gap buyers must hear: no calendar booking

Here is the limitation that decides the purchase. Front Office emails call details after every call — but its homepage describes no calendar booking and no CRM sync. The vendor page is silent on both, so until the vendor says otherwise, treat this as email summaries, not a booking engine.

That makes the fit clear:

  • Good fit: a shop whose bottleneck is after-hours triage — the emergency call at 9:42 PM that currently hits voicemail, where fewer than 3% of callers leave a message (a vendor-cited Invoca figure, not independent research). You get the details by email and call back first thing.
  • Bad fit: a shop that wants the call to end with a job on the calendar. For that, you need a booking-capable plan — and you should confirm the booking behavior in writing before buying any receptionist, not just this one.

Front Office’s own homepage cites three vendor-sourced stats — “85% of callers who don’t get an answer never call back” (Callsetter 2026), “one missed service call is worth $300–$1,200 in work” (Brilo AI 2026) — labeled here as vendor-cited, not measured by us. The underlying point stands without the stats: an unanswered emergency call is a lost job, and voicemail recovers almost none of them.

The bottom line

Front Office is the receptionist for the contractor who loses after-hours emergency calls and wants trade-specific triage plus an email, not a booking engine — at a flat $299–$449 that never surprises you. If booking is on your must-have list, this is not your plan, and the premium over Goodcall Starter ($79) Try Goodcall buys setup and certainty, not minutes. Try the 7-day trial on your own line, listen to the recorded test call, and confirm the booking question directly before you commit.

Frequently asked questions

How much does Front Office cost?

Front Office lists two flat monthly plans on its own homepage, verified November 1, 2026: Starter at $299 per month for up to 60 calls, and Full Front Office at $449 per month for 61 to 200 calls. There are no per-minute or per-call charges. A 7-day trial runs free on the owner's own line, and founding clients pay no setup fee and lock their rate for 24 months.

Does Front Office book appointments into a calendar?

Not as far as its own homepage describes. After every call it emails the owner the caller's details — name, number, address, and problem. The page describes no calendar booking and no CRM sync, so treat it as email summaries, not a booking engine, until the vendor says otherwise. If booking is a must-have, ask Front Office directly before buying.

What trades is Front Office built for?

Per its own homepage: HVAC, plumbing, electrical, roofing, garage doors, restoration, pest control, septic and drain, appliance repair, and generators. Setup runs from eight answers — hours, service-call fee, service area, what counts as an emergency, and the rest — and the shop hears a recorded test call and approves it before go-live.

How does Front Office compare with Goodcall for a contractor?

Goodcall Starter is $79 per month for 100 unique callers with unlimited minutes, so Front Office at $299 costs roughly four times as much at the entry tier. What the premium buys is trade-specific setup and a flat rate with no metering — not more minutes. For a shop whose bottleneck is after-hours emergency triage rather than scheduling, that trade may be worth it; for a shop that wants bookings, Goodcall or a booking-capable plan fits better.
