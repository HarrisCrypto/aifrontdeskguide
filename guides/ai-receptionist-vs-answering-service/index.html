AI Receptionist vs. Answering Service: Which Should Your Business Use?

AI receptionist vs traditional answering service compared honestly: cost ($25–$300/mo vs $300–$2,100/mo), hours, capabilities, and when a human still wins. Verified 2026 pricing.

By Jason O’Keefe·October 12, 2026

How we checked this guide: pricing figures were read from the vendors’ own pricing pages on October 13, 2026. This page contains affiliate links; see our affiliate disclosure.

The short answer

An AI receptionist is software that answers your business line and handles the call itself — greeting, questions, booking, messages — 24/7, for roughly $25–$300/month. A traditional answering service is a team of human agents who answer your calls following your script, for roughly $300–$2,100/month. The AI is cheaper and never sleeps; the human reads the room. Most small businesses are choosing AI for the routine work and keeping humans (or a hybrid) for the moments that need judgment.

The cost gap is the story

Run the same month through both. Say your business gets 300 calls a month:

  • AI: Upfirst’s Pro plan covers 300 calls for $159.95/month. Goodcall’s Growth plan covers ~750 calls for $129/month. Smith.ai’s AI Pro starts at $150/month.
  • Human: Smith.ai’s own live-receptionist Pro plan — 300 calls, answered by real people — is $2,100/month. At per-minute rates ($1.50–$1.90), 300 five-minute calls is $2,250–$2,850.

Same phone line, roughly a 10x cost difference. That gap is why this comparison exists at all. The question isn’t whether AI is cheaper — it’s whether the cheaper thing handles your calls well enough.

When the human still wins

Be honest about your call mix. A human answering service is still the right call when:

  • Calls are emotionally loaded. A flooded basement at 2 a.m., a disputed bill, a medical scare — distressed callers want a person. AI handles the informational part; it doesn’t do empathy convincingly yet.
  • Judgment calls are the job. “Is this an emergency worth waking the on-call tech?” is a decision, not a script. Humans make it; AI follows the rule you wrote, which is only as good as your rule.
  • Your callers expect a person. Some clientele — older demographics, high-trust professions — hear a synthetic voice and hang up. Know your customers.
  • Compliance is strict. Regulated intake (legal, medical, financial) sometimes needs a trained human’s discretion and a clear chain of custody. Get compliance terms in writing from any vendor before routing regulated calls through them.

When AI wins outright

  • After-hours and overflow. This is the slam dunk. The calls you’re currently sending to voicemail — the ones where half of callers hang up without leaving a message — get answered, booked, and summarized by morning. No shift differential, no holiday pay.
  • High-volume, routine calls. “What are your hours?” “Do you service my area?” “Can I book Tuesday?” AI answers these identically every time, at 3 a.m., during the tenth simultaneous call.
  • Tight budgets. If the alternative to AI is voicemail (not a $2,100/month human service), AI isn’t competing with humans — it’s competing with nothing, and nothing loses every time.
  • Consistency. The AI never forgets to ask the qualifying question, never misspells the caller’s name in the message, never has an off day.

The hybrid option: AI first, human backup

You don’t have to choose. Smith.ai’s AI receptionist can transfer a call to a live North America-based receptionist when the conversation goes beyond what it should handle — their own pricing page describes this as the core of the product. AI takes the routine 90%; a person catches the tricky 10%. You pay AI rates for most calls and only lean on the human layer when it matters.

This is also the shape of the industry’s direction: pure-human services keep raising prices (labor isn’t getting cheaper), pure-AI keeps getting better at the routine work, and the winners blend them.

How to decide: a 5-minute test

  1. Pull last month’s calls. How many? How long? What were they about?
  2. Sort them: routine (hours, booking, quotes, messages) vs. judgment (complaints, emergencies, complex intake).
  3. If 80%+ is routine: start with AI. A 14-day free trial (Upfirst) or a free tier (Smith.ai, 25 calls/month) costs you nothing to test on your real line.
  4. If judgment calls dominate: price a human service — or go hybrid — and compare against what a missed high-value call costs you.
  5. Test with real calls, not demos. Forward your actual line for two weeks. Read the transcripts. The transcripts tell you everything: did it ask the right questions? Did it book correctly? Would you have been embarrassed if that was your employee?

The bottom line

For most small businesses, the AI receptionist has crossed the “good enough” line for routine calls at a tenth of the cost — and the free trials mean you can verify that on your own line before spending a dollar. Keep humans (or a hybrid like Smith.ai’s) for the calls where judgment and empathy are the product. The most expensive option isn’t AI or human — it’s voicemail.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between an AI receptionist and an answering service?

An AI receptionist is software that answers calls, holds conversations, books appointments, and takes messages 24/7, typically for $25–$300/month. A traditional answering service uses human agents to answer and route calls, typically for $300–$2,100/month. The AI never sleeps and costs less; the human handles nuance, emotion, and complex judgment better.

How much does an answering service cost compared to an AI receptionist?

Human answering services run about $1.50–$1.90 per minute (per Goodcall’s own pricing page), and Smith.ai’s own live-receptionist plans run $300/month for 30 calls to $2,100/month for 300 calls. AI receptionists run $25–$300/month. For the same 300 calls, AI costs roughly a tenth of human answering.

Can an AI receptionist replace a human answering service completely?

For routine calls — FAQs, appointment booking, message taking, lead capture — yes, and most businesses won’t miss the human. For emotionally charged calls (an angry customer, a distressed patient, a complex complaint), a human still handles it better. Hybrid options exist: Smith.ai’s AI can transfer a call to a live North America-based receptionist when it gets stuck.

Do answering services work after hours?

Most 24/7 answering services do cover nights and weekends — that’s their selling point — but after-hours coverage sometimes costs extra or sits on higher tiers. AI receptionists include 24/7 by default on every plan we checked; there is no “after-hours surcharge” because there are no shifts.

Which is better for a small business on a budget?

An AI receptionist, by a wide margin on cost. Entry AI plans start at $24.95/month (Upfirst) with a free tier available (Smith.ai, 25 calls/month). The cheapest human answering we verified is $300/month for 30 calls. If budget is the constraint, AI wins; if call complexity is the constraint, consider human or hybrid.
