Best AI Receptionist for Plumbers in 2026: Goodcall vs Vapi, Honestly Compared

Choosing an AI receptionist for a plumbing business? A plumbing-specific, honest comparison of Goodcall and Vapi — after-hours emergencies, dispatch, long calls, price shoppers — plus which shops each one fits. Prices verified October 2026.

By Jason O’Keefe·October 12, 2026·Updated October 17, 2026

How we checked this guide: Goodcall and Vapi pricing below was read from their own pricing pages and current third-party pricing breakdowns on October 16, 2026, and the Goodcall plans re-verified on October 18, 2026 — prices change, so confirm before you buy. Disclosure: this site is run by the founder of OplyAI, an AI front-desk company. This page contains affiliate links; see our affiliate disclosure.

The short answer

A plumbing business lives or dies on the phone: emergency calls come at all hours, callers who hit voicemail call the next plumber, and every missed after-hours call is a job for a competitor. An AI receptionist answers every call 24/7, sorts emergencies from routine work, transfers urgent calls to the on-call tech, and books everything else.

Of the services you can buy through this site, the honest comparison comes down to two very different products: Goodcall, a self-serve AI receptionist that bills per unique caller with unlimited minutes (from $79/month), and Vapi, a developer platform for building voice agents ($0.05/minute orchestration plus provider costs passed through). Most plumbing shops want the first one. The second one is for shops with a developer or an agency doing the build. We’ll show you exactly why — and run the math.

What plumbers specifically need

Generic “AI receptionist” advice misses the trade. Here’s what actually matters on a plumbing line:

  1. After-hours is the whole game. Burst pipes don’t wait for business hours. Whatever you buy must answer at 2 a.m. on a Sunday — and not charge extra for it.
  2. Emergency triage, not just message-taking. “No hot water” can wait until morning; “sewage backing into the house” can’t. You need to define the emergency rules and have urgent calls transferred live to whoever’s on call — not dumped into a message queue you’ll read at 7 a.m.
  3. Long calls shouldn’t spike the bill. A panicked homeowner describing a flood talks for ten minutes. On per-minute billing, that’s ten metered minutes; on per-caller billing, it’s one unit. The billing model matters more for plumbers than for most businesses.
  4. Spam is brutal on trade lines. Contractor numbers end up on every robocall and lead-seller list. A vendor that bills you for spam is taxing you for being listed.
  5. Booking, not just notes. “We’ll call you back” loses to “we’ve got a tech there between 9 and 11.” Calendar booking turns the call into a job.

Goodcall — the pick for most plumbing shops Try Goodcall

Why it fits plumbers: Goodcall bills per unique customer per month — one price per distinct phone number, with unlimited minutes. A 20-minute emergency intake costs exactly the same as a 30-second wrong number, and a repeat caller who phones three times in a month counts once. For a trade where calls are long and customers call back, this is the most predictable bill in the category.

Current pricing (verified October 18, 2026): Starter $79/month for 100 unique customers, Growth $129/month for 500, Scale $299/month for 2,000 — then 79¢ per extra customer on Starter, 26¢ on Growth, 15¢ on Scale over your cap. No annual discount is advertised on the pricing page as of this update. There is no per-minute meter running, ever.

Other things that matter on a plumbing line: it answers 24/7, it can pull your hours and services from your Google Business Profile so the agent knows the basics on day one, and you can script call-handling rules (“logic flows” — 1 on Starter, 3 on Growth, 25 on Scale) for things like service areas and pricing questions. When the AI can’t resolve something, it can text the caller a link to your booking page.

Watch out for: it’s AI-only — there is no human backup tier if a call goes sideways. A marketing push that brings lots of new callers can push you into overage (79¢ each on Starter), so model your busiest month, not your average one. And integrations mostly run through Zapier rather than native trade-CRM connections — confirm your calendar and dispatch workflow connects before you commit.

Rough cost at 100 unique callers/month: $79 flat. A 15-minute average call length changes nothing — that’s the point.

Vapi — the pick only if you have a builder Try Vapi

What it actually is: Vapi is not a receptionist you buy — it’s a developer platform for building voice agents. You (or an agency you hire) assemble the agent: you pick the speech-to-text provider, the language model, the voice, and the phone setup, and Vapi orchestrates the call. That means total control — and total responsibility for building, testing, and maintaining it.

Pricing, honestly: Vapi charges $0.05 per minute for orchestration, and every provider cost underneath (transcription, model, voice, telephony) is passed through at cost. Vapi’s own pricing calculator puts a typical stack at roughly $0.08–$0.13 per minute all-in; independent breakdowns estimate $0.07–$0.25 per minute depending on model and voice choices. There are also per-line and per-number fees on some plans (around $10/month per extra concurrent line and ~$2 per phone number have been reported — verify current plan packaging on their pricing page, as plan names have shifted during 2026).

Why a plumber might consider it: if you already work with an agency or have technical help, a Vapi build can be tuned exactly to your trade — your emergency rules, your dispatch workflow, your voice. At 400 minutes a month, the platform cost can land around $30–$100 — cheap if the build labor is already covered.

Why most plumbers shouldn’t: the sticker price assumes somebody has already built the agent and keeps it running. If that somebody is you, on nights and weekends, between jobs — buy the finished product instead. A per-minute meter also punishes exactly what plumbing lines do: long emergency calls. Run 1,000 minutes in a freeze month and you’re at $80–$250 in platform cost before anyone’s time.

The honest test: if you don’t have a developer or agency on speed dial, close the Vapi tab and buy Goodcall. If you do, Vapi is the most flexible voice platform in the category.

What to watch out for (plumber-specific)

  • “AI receptionist” that can’t transfer live. If emergencies can’t be patched to the on-call tech in real time, it’s a message-taker with good marketing. Verify live transfer exists on your plan.
  • After-hours routing rules. Can you set “ring the on-call cell for emergencies, book everything else”? If the vendor can’t do time-and-urgency routing, keep shopping.
  • The overage trap at 2 a.m. Your busiest months are winter freezes and summer storms — exactly when per-minute and per-caller overages spike. Model your peak month, not your average one.
  • Who trains it on plumbing. “Burst pipe,” “sewage ejector,” “water heater pilot” — the AI needs your trade’s vocabulary in its knowledge base. Whoever builds it (you or the vendor) has to do this work; there’s no shortcut.
  • Test with real emergency calls. Have a friend call at 11 p.m. with a fake burst pipe. Read the transcript. Did it follow your emergency rule? Did it transfer? This 10-minute test tells you more than any demo.

The bottom line

For most plumbing shops, this is a short decision: Goodcall if you want a finished product with a predictable bill — $79/month covers 100 unique callers no matter how long they talk Try Goodcall. Vapi only if you have a builder and want maximum control at platform cost Try Vapi. Either way, test it on your real line for two weeks and read the transcripts before you commit. The transcripts will tell you which one to keep.

Frequently asked questions

Do plumbers need an AI receptionist?

Most do, because plumbing demand is spiky and after-hours. Emergency calls come at 2 a.m., and callers who reach voicemail usually call the next plumber on the list. An AI receptionist answers every call, books the job, and texts you the summary — far cheaper than a human answering service.

What should a plumber look for in an AI receptionist?

Four things: 24/7 answering with no after-hours surcharge, live call transfer to the on-call tech for true emergencies, appointment booking into your calendar, and billing that does not punish you for long emergency calls. Check the overage terms before you sign.

Is Goodcall good for plumbing companies?

It fits shops whose calls run long. Goodcall bills per unique caller per month ($79 for 100 callers) with unlimited minutes, so a 20-minute emergency intake costs the same as a 30-second wrong number. Verify current plans on their pricing page before buying.

Is Vapi a good fit for a plumber?

Only if you have a developer or an agency building it. Vapi is a voice-AI platform ($0.05/min orchestration plus provider costs passed through) — you assemble the agent yourself. Most plumbing shops are better served by a self-serve product like Goodcall.

Can an AI receptionist tell an emergency from a routine call?

It follows the rules you set: you define what counts as an emergency (no water, sewage backup, burst pipe) and what happens next — transfer to the on-call tech immediately, or book next-day service. It will not improvise judgment, so write the rules carefully and test them with real calls.

Will customers hang up on an AI voice?

Some might, especially on emergency calls where they want a person fast. That is why live transfer matters: the AI qualifies the call in seconds and patches emergencies straight through to a human. Test any service on your real line for two weeks and read the transcripts before committing.
